Charter

An internal AI system that reads a Business Requirements Document and drafts a scored, build-ready delivery plan — engineering plan, schedule, architecture, proof-of-concept scope, and technology options — in about ten minutes.

Live · Azure, Central India Single-instance internal tool Powered by OpenAI GPT‑4.1

The problem

Turning a BRD into a delivery plan is slow, manual, and inconsistent

Once a Business Requirements Document is signed off, an Engineering Manager still has to hand-produce five separate artifacts before a team can start: a phased plan, an effort and schedule estimate, a solution architecture, a proof-of-concept scope, and a technology recommendation. Each depends on the others, each should reflect the organization's own patterns and standards, and today none of that is checked before it lands on a manager's desk.

That work typically takes days, its quality depends on who happens to write it, and there is no consistent way to confirm a plan actually covers every requirement or matches how the organization has built similar things before. Charter automates the first draft of that work and grounds it in the organization's own knowledge — then scores it before a person ever sees it.

How it works

One BRD in, a scored delivery plan out

Charter is not one model answering a prompt — it's a coordinator, five specialists, and a reviewer, wired as a graph with one deliberate loop back into itself.

What makes it reliable

Four disciplines, working together

01

Ingestion & parsing

Reads the uploaded document, splits it into sections, and classifies every sentence as functional, non-functional, a constraint, or an assumption — flagging anything ambiguous.

02

Knowledge grounding

Every agent retrieves relevant material from the organization's own past BRDs, templates, architecture patterns, and technology standards before it writes a word.

03

Multi-agent generation

Five specialists each produce one deliverable, building on each other's output — the schedule reads the plan's phases, the PoC reads the architecture's components.

04

Validation & evaluation

A Critic scores every deliverable against the organization's own bar and forces revisions — nothing reaches the Engineering Manager unreviewed.
